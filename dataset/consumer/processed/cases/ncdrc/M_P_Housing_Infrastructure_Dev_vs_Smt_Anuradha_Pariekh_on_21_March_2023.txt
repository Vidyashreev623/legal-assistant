 O R D E R
(Passed On 21.03.2023)                    The following order of the Commission was delivered by A. K.
Tiwari, Presiding Member:           
                   The opposite party/appellant M. P. Housing Board has filed this appeal
against the order dated 23.08.2017 passed in C.C.No.130/2014 by the District
Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission, Mandsaur whereby the complaint filed
by the complainant/respondent has been allowed.
2.                Facts of the case in short as stated by the complainant/respondent are that the opposite
party housing board advertised in Hindi newspaper 'Nai Duniya' dated 22.10.2010 regarding
scheme for 35 houses in Gandhi Nagar, Mandsaur.  It was self-financing scheme costing
Rs.12,59,000/- for a MIG house.  On the basis of said advertisement, the complainant after
depositing registration amount of Rs.1,25,200/- got herself registered and applied for a MIG house
under the scheme.  Thereafter she had deposited Rs.3,78,000/- as first instalment. It is alleged by
the complainant that despite depositing part payment of cost of the MIG house, Rs.5,03,200/-, the
opposite party committed deficiency in service in not allotting the MIG house to the complainant on
earlier rate Rs.12,59,000/- and by escalating the cost of the house. She therefore filed a complaint
before the District Commission alleging deficiency in service on part of opposite party-housing
board seeking relief of amount of Rs.5,03,200/- with interest @ 14% p.a. to be adjusted in cost of
the house along with compensation of Rs.15,00,000/-.
3.                The opposite party Housing Board resisted the complaint stating that after registration
tenders were invited for two group of constructions I-15/35 and II-20/35. Tenders were received for
construction of the 15 houses, however, no tender was received for construction of rest of the 20
houses. To expedite the work tender for 15 houses was accepted and construction was started. Since
the construction work of 15 houses was in progress, in the interest of applicants, it was decided to
allot the 15 houses to the applicants through lottery. The information of lottery for 15 houses was
sent to all 35 applicants including the complainant. The complainant could not be selected in the
open lottery process.  Since the construction work of rest 20 houses was started much after the
construction of 15 houses, and by that time cost of construction material was increased and
therefore, the cost of the house was revised to Rs.22,95,000/-. In the advertisement only estimated
cost of the house was given.  The complainant got registered herself for house and had deposited
registration fees, as also the first instalment but could not be selected in the lottery for first 15
houses. There is no deficiency in service on part of the opposite party-housing board. It is therefore
prayed that the complaint be dismissed.

5.                We have heard complainant/respondent and have gone through the written arguments
filed by the opposite party/appellant. Perused the record of the District Commission. 
 
6.                Learned counsel for the appellants-housing board in the written arguments has
submitted that the impugned order passed by the District Commission is against the facts and
settled principle of law and deserves to be set-aside. It is submitted that every housing scheme, its
execution, rules related to any scheme have statutory enforceability under the M.P.Housing and
Infrastructure Development Board Act, 1972. It clearly demonstrates that any rules/circulars
published/issued by the Board in relation to any housing scheme have legal enforceability and
binding on the persons, who have verified these rules and instructions issued time to time in
relation the scheme with their signature and application form. The District Commission failed to
consider this legal position. In a self-financing scheme costing plays an important role. An applicant
cannot be permitted to buy a house at a price which is less than the actual construction cost. The
disputes regarding escalation in price are not entertainable before the District Commission
constituted under the Consumer Protection Act, 1986 (for short the 'Act'). The scheme of the Board
is based on 'no profit no loss' basis and without any commitment of delivery of possession. The
complainant neither alleged nor proved that there has been any negligence, short comings or
inadequacy on part of Board. Reliance was placed on the decisions of the Hon'ble Supreme Court in
Kanpur Development Authority Vs Sheela Devi I (2004) CPJ 12 (SC), Bangalore Development
Authority Vs Syndicate Bank II (2007) CPJ 21 (SC), and decision of Hon'ble National Commission in
Sudha Nagla Vs Rajasthan Housing Board IV (2016) CPJ 63 (NC) and decision of this Commission
in K. B. Joshi Vs M. P. Housing Board, IV (2009) CPJ 442.
7.                Complainant/Respondent, who appears in person argued that the District Commission
after considering all aspects had passed a well-reasoned order as the opposite party has committed
deficiency in service in escalating the cost of the house and in not allotting and giving possession of
the house in rate as shown earlier, to the complainant. It is therefore prayed that the appeal be
dismissed.
8.                After hearing complainant/respondent and on perusal of record and written arguments
filed by the opposite party/appellant we find that for 35 MIG houses, applications were invited by
the opposite party and the complainant also applied for the same after depositing the registration
amount and thereafter first instalment. For the purposes of construction two tenders one for 15
houses and second for 20 houses were invited. Since the construction work of 15 houses was started,
therefore, in the interest of justice, open lottery system for 15 houses was conducted in which the
complainant was also participated, however, she could not be selected. The 15 applicants who were
selected in the lottery system were allotted 15 houses. Later, construction of 20 houses was started.

and by that time cost of construction material was increased and therefore, the cost of the house was

estimated cost and not of final cost. 
9.                The complainant got registered herself for house and had deposited registration fees, as
also the first instalment but could not be selected in the lottery for first 15 houses.  At the time of
registration a Rule Book "Nirdeshika" had already been given to the complainant which contains
rules describing the various conditions.  In the said rule book, in Rule-1, it is categorically
mentioned that the rates are fluctuating and the amended terms amended from time to time will be
binding on the applicant/allottee. In case of disagreement, the applicant/allottee is at liberty to
withdraw the deposited amount with interest. It is expected from the complainant that she/he may
get information regarding registration, allotment, possession of the scheme. 
10.              From the record we find that out of 35 applicants, the housing board after conducting
open lottery system allotted the 15 house to the applicants who were selected in the lottery. We do
not find that there was any discrimination on part of the housing board.
 
11.              So far as the cross appeal in form of cross objections filed by the complainant/respondent
under Order 41 Rule 22 of CPC are concerned, it is well settled principle that cross appeal in form of
cross objections does not lie. See the decision of the Hon'ble National Commission in EE (O&M)
Tamilnadu Electricity Board Vs K. R. Mani III (1995) CPJ 46 (NC)
12.              In Sheela Devi (supra) Hon'ble Supreme Court has held that "Each case is to be decided in
the facts and circumstances of the case in the light of the scheme framed and the terms and
conditions mentioned in the brochure." In Bangalore Development Authority (supra) the Hon'ble
Supreme Court has held that "Where the plot/flat/house has been allotted at a tentative or
provisional price, subject to final determination of price on completion of the project, the
Development Authority will be entitled to revise or increase the price."
13.              It is pertinent to mention here that the main issue involved in the matter is regarding
escalation of cost/pricing. We are in full agreement with the arguments of the opposite
party/appellant housing board in the written arguments that it is well settled that the Consumer
Forums cannot go into the question of pricing of houses/flats and hence we also refrain to deal with
the issued involved in the matter. 
   
14.              The Hon'ble National Commission in case of Managing Director, Cooperative Housing
Complex of Housefed, Punjab Vs Gurudev Singh Azad 2018 SCC OnLine NCDRC 74 in para 11 has
held:

Having regard to the catena of judgments of the Hon'ble Supreme Court, there is
force in the contention of the learned counsel for the Appellant that the 'Consumer
For a' do not have jurisdiction to decide the pricing of the Housing Sector.
 
15.              Similarly, the Hon'ble National Commission in case of Rasheed Ahmed Usmani and
Others Vs DLF Ltd 2019 SCC OnLine NCDRC 84. in para 442 has reproduced the law laid down by
the National Conmmission in earlier decision of (Three Member Bench) in Manohar Lal Sharma Vs
D. D. A. I (1994) CPJ 29 (NC) that:
"After adverting to the relevant decisions on the point this Commission held that the
question of pricing cannot be gone into by the Consumer Forums since the price of
the flats is not fixed by any law and that even if any excess charge has been collected
by way of price that will not constitute a ground                                         for contending
that there is a deficiency in service on the part of the opposite party."
   
16.              In Sudha Nagla (supra) the Hon'ble National Commission has held that question of
costing or price fixation of flats/houses or land by a public authority is not within the purview of
courts. Similar view has been taken by this Commission in First Appeal No.341/2013 and other
connected First Appeals (M.P.Housing & Infrastructure Development Board Vs Smt. Saroj Agrawal)
decided on 22.08.2022.
 
17.              In view of the aforesaid settled position of law, the impugned order passed by the District
Commission cannot be sustained and is hereby set-aside.
18.           In the result, the appeal is allowed.  However, no order as to costs.
