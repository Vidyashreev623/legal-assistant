at a higher rate whereas the Societies to whom the deposited money had not been refunded, the land
was allotted at the original price. The Honble Supreme Court in the Bangalore Development
Authoritys case (Supra) has held that where the land is allotted at the price prevailing at the time of
initial allotment, the Authority concerned is not liable to pay interest. It has been observed as
under:-
13. As already noticed, where the grievance is one of delay in delivery of possession
and the Development Authority delivers the house during the pendency of the
complaint at the agreed price, and such delivery is accepted by the
allottee-complainant, the question of awarding any interest on the price paid by him
from the date of deposit to date of delivery of possession does not arise. The allottee
who had the benefit of appreciation of price of the house, is not entitled to interest on
the price paid.
In this case, the 11 houses were delivered in 1977 at the agreed price (Rs.5.5 lacs per corner HIG
House and Rs.4.75 lacs per other HIG Houses). In view of it, the order of the Commission awarding
interest @ 18% p.a. on the price of the houses is unsustainable and liable to be set aside.
In Federations case (Supra), the Division Bench of the High Court of Delhi had directed the DDA to
pay the interest from the date of demand of refund by the Society. It was specifically held in the said
judgment that the interest would be payable only in the cases where re-allotment of land had not
been made. In the cases where the Societies were re-allotted the land, interest was not required to be
paid. Relevant observations of the High Court of Delhi in Federations case read as under:-
It is no doubt true that the payment of interest was ordered in the case of Sriniketan
Co-op. Societys case (Supra) but the important distinction between the present case
and that of Sriniketans case is that here in the letters of allotment itself it was stated
that on the allotment being cancelled no interest would be payable. Therefore, there
can be no claim of interest by the societies w.e.f. the date of their deposit.
Furthermore, the Supreme Court contemplated interest not being awarded to those
societies who were re-allotted land. Before us there is no society who had deposited
the money and has not been re-allotted land. It would, however, be just and fair that
if a demand for refund is made on the DDA then the DDA, if it does not re-pay the
money within a reasonable time, should be bound to pay some interest. We are
informed that some of the co-operative Societies had written to the DDA for refund of
money. If the DDA did not refund the money on its own then it would be unfair and it
would not be entitled to take benefit of the aforesaid clause 6 and escape the liability
to pay interest when a demand for refund of the money is specifically made on it by a
co-operative society. In such a case, in our opinion the DDA would be liable to pay
interest @ 12% p.a., which rate was awarded by the Supreme Court in Sriniketan
Co-operative Societys case (supra) w.e.f the date the notice of demand was served on
the DDA till the date of payment. This payment of interest will only be made to those
societies to whom no re-allotment of land has been made. (Emphasis supplied) We

Ltd. to whom the interest had been paid, the land was re-allotted at the higher price
than the initial offer made to them.
Moreover, the DDA has accepted the order passed by the State Commission in complaint case
No.C-44/97. In the absence of any appeal by the DDA, the order passed by the State Commission in
complaint case No. C-44/97cannot be interfered. In none of the cases cited by the Complainants the
point as to whether the interest is to be paid to the Societies to whom the land had been allotted, has
been considered. In view of this, the ratio of those judgments would not be applicable to the facts of
the present cases.
The First Appeal Nos. 280/05 and 298/05 filed by the Complainant Societies seeking enhancement
of the compensation are dismissed in view of the judgments of the Supreme Court in Bangalore
Development Authoritys case (Supra) and the Division Bench of High Court of Delhi in Federations
case(Supra).
In view of what has been stated above, the First Appeal Nos. 355/05 and 356/05 filed by the DDA
for setting aside the orders passed by the State Commission are liable to be accepted. But in the
peculiar facts and circumstances of these cases, direction issued by the State Commission to pay
lump-sum compensation of Rs.1,50,000/- in FA No.280/05 and Rs.2,00,000/- in FA No. 298/05 is
maintained as the DDA had been taking inconsistent stand. DDA had paid interest to some of the
Societies to whom the land was re-allotted whereas the Complainant Societies had not been paid the
interest though allotted the land at the original price. Even after paying the interest and re-allotment
of land, DDA had charged different prices from the different Societies as is evident from the orders
passed by the State Commission. From Daya Nand Co-op. Group Housing Society Ltd., it had
charged Rs.2,033.61 per sq. mtr. whereas from Khosla Compressor Co-op. Group Housing Society
Ltd., DDA had charged Rs.1,861.65 per sq. mtr. These Societies had been charged higher rate per
square metre as the DDA had paid the interest to them on the deposited amount. Universal
Brotherhood Co-op. Group Housing Society Ltd. and the Rajnigandha Co-operative Group Housing
Society Ltd. (Appellants in FA No.280/05 and 295/05 respectively) were allotted the land @
Rs.1650.65 per sq. mtr. on the ground that no interest had been paid to them. The nexus and the
rationale of charging different prices from the Societies to whom the land was given at a lower price
but no interest was paid and charging of higher price because the interest had been paid to those
Societies, has not been explained.
In view of the reasons stated above, although in principle, we are deciding that the DDA is not liable
to pay interest on re-allotment of land at the original rate but decline to interfere with the direction
given by the State Commission to pay the lump-sum compensation of Rs.1,50,000/- in FA
No.280/05 and Rs.2,00,000/- in FA No.298/05 as the DDA has been taking adhoc and inconsistent
stand thereby causing a heart burning to the Complainant Societies. The First Appeals are disposed
of in the above manner. No order as to costs.
